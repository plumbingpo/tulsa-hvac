How Broken Arrow Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

The age of your equipment plays a big role. With regular service, central air typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new system during the worst heat wave of the year.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a miserable night.
